Shyamal Dharia

Senior Research Assistant · The University of Winnipeg

I am a Senior Research Assistant at The University of Winnipeg, working with Dr. Camilo Valderrama and Dr. Stephen D. Smith. I work on machine learning for brain data, building representations that generalize across datasets, sites, and recording conditions, and models efficient enough to run on edge hardware.

  • Self-supervised learning — joint-embedding predictive architectures (JEPA) for zero-shot harmonization of multi-site neuroimaging data.
  • Foundation models — JEPA-based pretraining for wearable EEG devices, supporting scalable and data-efficient downstream learning.
  • Efficient inference — differentiable logic gate networks for low-latency EEG classification on edge devices.

I completed my MSc in Applied Computer Science at the University of Winnipeg and previously earned a BEng in Electronics and Communication Engineering from Gujarat Technological University.

Differentiable Logic Gate Networks for Low-Latency EEG Classification on Edge Devices

Dharia, S. Y., Smith, S. D., Valderrama Cuadros, C. E.

Canadian AI Conference, 2026 · Best Paper Award · Oral

Compiles EEG classifiers into Boolean circuits that run on bitwise CPU operations: 80.2% macro-F1 on dementia screening (6.8% above a matched MLP) and up to 2.9× faster inference on a 7 W Jetson Orin Nano.

Exploring the interaction of APOE-ε4 and PICALM rs3851179 with dynamic functional connectivity in healthy middle-aged adults at risk for Alzheimer's disease

Dharia, S., Valderrama, C. E., Liu, Q., Smith, S. D.

Journal of Neural Engineering, 2026

Cognitively healthy adults carrying APOE-ε4 spend significantly less time in left-hemisphere hub connectivity states in resting-state fMRI, a candidate marker of Alzheimer's risk before symptoms appear.

A Novel Approach for the Early Identification of Genetic Risk Factors for Alzheimer's Disease Using EEG and Psychometric Data

Dharia, S. Y., Liu, Q., Smith, S. D., Valderrama Cuadros, C. E.

IEEE Journal of Biomedical and Health Informatics, 2025

A CNN on EEG and psychometric scores predicts Alzheimer's genetic risk without a genetic test (72.2% F1 for non-carriers vs. APOE-ε4/PICALM risk carriers); Grad-CAM shows EEG features carry most of the signal.

Figure note: the illustration featuring a face with an EEG headset was designed by Vansh Patel (arguably the best-designed part of the paper).

Fractal Dimension of Resting-State EEG as a Biomarker for Autonomous Sensory Meridian Response (ASMR)

Dharia, S. Y., Valderrama Cuadros, C. E., Liu, Q., Fredborg, B. K., Desroches, A. S., Smith, S. D.

IEEE Journal of Biomedical and Health Informatics, 2025 · Oral at BHI 2025

ASMR-sensitive people show a distinct resting-state EEG signature (lower delta/theta and higher alpha fractal complexity), which a Transformer uses to identify them at 82.6% F1.

Biomedical Signal Processing and Control, 2025

Cross-attention fusion of connectivity (dPTE) and spectral (DE) features detects Alzheimer's disease from just 6 EEG channels at 84.9% F1, comparable to full-channel state of the art.
